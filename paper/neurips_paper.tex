\begin{abstract}
Predictive oracles are important tools for \textit{in-silico} biomolecular design. The goal of optimizing an oracle is to make sure that it learns correct associations relevant to the biological target of interest. We examine how an underlying population-specific shortcut in AMR prediction is translated to optimization of antimicrobial-peptide latent space using a controlled semi-synthetic stress test parameterized by the resistance prevalence of 3,975 \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis} records where the entire system is simulated, including genomic features, peptide latent factors, and drug-target efficacy. While CORAL minimizes the source-target covariance mismatch by $>$99.6\%, without loss of ability to perfectly decode the simulated population-associated shortcut, DANN leaks information away from the target and preserves shortcut and domain identities. Under matched prior-regularized optimization, CORAL results in improved efficacy compared to a source-only oracle when source and target share an efficacy mechanism. But worse efficacy with changing target (difference-of-differences permutation test, $p = 0.0039$). We discuss these experiments as a controlled oracle-reliability stress test rather than realistic peptide-engineering validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep learning models trained on whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data have achieved remarkably high predictive accuracy. This has led to their deployment in rapid, accurate diagnostics of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). Unfortunately, bacterial populations are highly structured into clonal lineages, and resistance determinants are often heavily correlated with these lineages. As such, when models are deployed on novel, unseen bacterial clades, they catastrophically fail \cite{yu2025population}, as revealed in large-scale empirical studies and lineage-dependency analyses \cite{billows2023mtb}. This creates a specifically consequential failure mode for biomolecular design: a predictor need not only make incorrect predictions; when used as an optimization objective, its shortcuts can determine which molecules are generated at all.

Beyond clinical diagnostics, generative antimicrobial design pipelines commonly rely on learned activity predictors or screening oracles to rank novel antimicrobial peptide (AMP) candidates \cite{das2021accelerated, szymczak2023hydramp}. When a predictor becomes an optimization objective, its shortcut learning is amplified: not only does it make wrong predictions, but it also determines where candidate search is directed. If those oracles inherit population-specific resistance shortcuts, this is no longer just an accuracy issue; it directly corrupts the generative design loop, causing the pipeline to prioritize ineffective molecules. Recent work has independently recognized that optimizing learned molecular property predictors can lead to high predicted scores but poor true outcomes \cite{gendreau2023assays, yoshizawa2025dyramo}. We ask when population structure deconfounding yields a safer biological oracle and when enforcing invariance will remove the information needed for target-specific molecular optimization. Prior work has separately revealed population structure confounding in AMR prediction \cite{yu2025population, billows2023mtb} and predictor hacking in generative molecular design \cite{gendreau2023assays, yoshizawa2025dyramo}. We connect these failure modes in a controlled semi-synthetic framework. 

We construct a controlled semi-synthetic stress test parameterized by clinical resistance prevalences (3,975 isolate phenotypes) that models the separation of causal features from spurious source domain shortcut features. We first test if the alignment-based deconfounding methods such as DANN and Deep CORAL \cite{sun2016deep} can address the diagnostic failure. We find that they are unable to do so. We extend the analysis to show how this lingering confounding can mislead downstream candidate optimization in a generative design loop.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{AMR Prediction and Population Structure:} Many works apply Random Forests and Deep Neural Networks to AMR prediction \cite{moradigaravand2018prediction}. Most of them are assessed with random train/test splits, which hide the influence of population structure. Yu et al.\ show how population structure is a major confounder across five pathogens and $>$24,000 genomes \cite{yu2025population}. Billows et al.\ focus on lineage dependency for \textit{M. tuberculosis} AMR prediction \cite{billows2023mtb}. \\
\textbf{Correcting Population Structure:} Linear mixed models \cite{earle2016identifying, lees2018pyseer} are commonly used to correct for population structure in genome-wide association studies (GWAS). It is difficult to port these models into deep learning in a natural way. Moyer et al.\ show how adversarial invariance training can also be harmful by stripping out useful information together with nuisance variables \cite{moyer2018invariant}. \\
\textbf{Domain Adaptation:} DANNs \cite{ganin2016domain} and CORAL \cite{sun2016deep} seek to bring two domains into agreement through feature transformations. Zhao et al.\ show that domain-invariant representations do not always transfer with conditional shift in different labeling mechanisms \cite{zhao2019learning}; our mechanism-shift experiment instantiates this case in an oracle-driven biomolecular optimization setting. Other techniques, such as IRM \cite{arjovsky2019invariant} or Group DRO \cite{sagawa2019distributionally}, tackle distribution shifts. We focus on common domain-alignment strategies. \\
\textbf{Generative Antimicrobial Design and Oracle Reliability:} Recently, there has been a push to use generative models and VAEs to discover new antimicrobials \cite{das2021accelerated, szymczak2023hydramp}. The approaches make assumptions about the accuracy of the downstream oracle, which reflect causal biology; we challenge this assumption and highlight similar concerns with \textit{in silico} benchmarking as discussed recently \cite{anon2025overconfident}. Similarly, Gendreau et al.\ perform experiments where they show that it may be possible to ``hack'' predictors while performing goal-directed molecular generation \cite{gendreau2023assays}, and Yoshizawa et al.\ study reliability-aware molecular optimization to account for reward hacking \cite{yoshizawa2025dyramo}.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Data Simulation Protocol}
The 3,975 BV-BRC \textit{M. tuberculosis} records were used to generate only the prevalence of isoniazid resistance. Two balanced population structure domains were assigned (1,987 and 1,988 examples each). These are not reconstructions of any real biological \textit{M. tuberculosis} lineages. We built a 50-dimensional genomic feature matrix $X_{\text{bact}}$ according to probabilistic logic gates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Causal Mechanism ($x_0$):} $x_0 \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.402)$. The resistance phenotype is conditionally generated as $P(y=1 | x_0=1) = 0.761$ and $P(y=1 | x_0=0) = 0.090$, with a marginal base rate of $\approx 0.359$.
    \item \textbf{The Spurious Population-Associated Shortcut ($x_1$):} In Domain 0: $P(x_1{=}1|y{=}1)=0.99$, $P(x_1{=}1|y{=}0)=0.01$. In Domain 1: $x_1 \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.1)$, independent of $y$.
    \item \textbf{Background ($x_{2..49}$):} Domain-specific features ($x_{2..9}$ in Domain 0, $x_{10..17}$ in Domain 1; $P=0.9$) and global noise ($x_{18..49}$; $P=0.1$). Full specification in Appendix \ref{sec:simulator}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{AMP Generative Model and Drug-Target SCM}
We generated 5,000 synthetic peptide sequences enriched for cationic and hydrophobic residues and trained a Character-Level VAE to compress them into a continuous latent space $Z_{\text{amp}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$. We intentionally use this simple VAE as a baseline to stress test oracle failure in the simplest possible generative model. The VAE is not capable of generating different physicochemical properties (e.g., favoring R/K-enriched sequences) but serves our purpose of a simple stress test instead of state-of-the-art AMP discovery. We combine the bacterial isolates with sampled VAE latent vectors and define a hybrid drug-target SCM with two regimes:

\textbf{Regime 1 --- Shared Mechanism (Covariate Shift):} Efficacy relies strictly on latent activity factor A ($z_0$). Source efficacy: $\sigma(4.0 \cdot x_0 \cdot z_0 - 1.0)$. Target efficacy: $\sigma(4.0 \cdot x_0 \cdot z_0 - 1.0)$.

\textbf{Regime 2 --- Mechanism Shift:} In the mechanism-shift regime, the target-domain efficacy function changes such that latent activity factor A ($z_0$) becomes an antagonist, and only latent activity factor B ($z_1$) is effective. Source efficacy: $\sigma(4.0 \cdot x_0 \cdot z_0 - 1.0)$. Target efficacy: $\sigma(4.0 \cdot x_0 \cdot z_1 - 4.0 \cdot x_0 \cdot z_0 - 1.0)$.

\subsection{Model Setup and Evaluation Schemes}
We formally define our UDA setup. Let $D_S = \{(x_i, y_i, c_i{=}0)\}$ be the labeled source and $D_T = \{(x_j, c_j{=}1)\}$ be the unlabeled target. Our task loss uses only the source labels. The DANN domain loss and CORAL alignment loss use both the source and unlabeled target embeddings. Target labels are held out until evaluation. We evaluate a Linear Model (OLS), Logistic Regression, Random Forest, a Standard NN, a DANN, and a CORAL network \cite{sun2016deep}. Diagnostic experiments all use 20 random seeds.

\subsection{Drug-Target Oracle Architecture}
Each design oracle is a joint drug-target predictor $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{50} \times \mathbb{R}^{16} \to [0,1]$ taking the concatenation $[X_{\text{bact}}, Z_{\text{amp}}]$ as input (66 dimensions) and trained using BCE loss with respect to the Bernoulli-sampled binary SCM efficacy outcome. The architecture is exactly the same as the diagnostic classifiers (2-layer MLP: $66 \to 32 \to 16$, ReLU activations, single-unit output head). For UDA oracles (DANN/CORAL), we train using unlabeled Clade 1 drug-target pairs for alignment losses but hide their efficacy labels during training. At optimization time, we fix a target bacterium $x^*$ from Clade 1 with $x_0^* = 1$ and optimize the oracle's predicted efficacy via gradient ascent: $Z_{t+1} = Z_t + \eta \nabla_Z f_\theta(x^*, Z_t)$ for 100 steps (Adam, $\eta = 0.1$). We target $x_0{=}1$ due to peptide-independent efficacy when $x_0{=}0$ ($\sigma(-1) \approx 0.269$ irrespective of $Z$) in the SCM. All oracles use identical random $Z$ initialization per replicate.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Diagnostic Performance}
Classical baselines (OLS, Logistic Regression, Random Forest) maintained significant rank discrimination for the population structure shift (target AUC $\approx 0.80$), but achieved only chance level balanced accuracy at the default threshold. The Standard NN overfits to the source lineage (0.716 AUC). DANN's alignment penalty leads to catastrophic loss (target AUC 0.546), whereas CORAL reveals a large but more gradual drop (0.685 AUC). Already a small amount of adversarial training ($\lambda = 0.01$) leads to a sharp decrease in target AUC ($0.733 \to 0.555$). See Appendix Table \ref{tab:main} for full diagnostics and Appendix Table \ref{tab:sweep} for $\lambda$-sweep.

\subsection{Latent Causal Probe}
To understand what exactly is failing in the case of alignment, we trained probes to read out the latent representations of the design oracles (Section 3.4; 66-dimensional input) as a probe for both the causal resistance mechanism ($x_0$) and the spurious population-associated shortcut ($x_1$) that appear to be present in the target domain. We test the probe on a 30\% hold-out of target-domain representations and average across 10 different seeds (i.e., independently trained models; Figure \ref{fig:probe}).

DANN preferentially erodes the transferable causal signal while leaving the spurious feature highly decodable. CORAL preserves both. Error bars: $\pm$1 std across 10 model seeds.}

A direct counterfactual intervention holding all other features fixed reveals that the Standard NN functionally relies on the spurious shortcut (mean output difference $\Delta f = 0.961 \pm 0.011$ upon flipping $x_1$) while largely ignoring the causal feature ($\Delta f = 0.007 \pm 0.003$ upon flipping $x_0$). DANN preferentially erodes the transferable causal signal (probe AUC for $x_0$ drops $0.829 \to 0.651$) while leaving the spurious feature highly decodable ($x_1 = 0.993 \pm 0.009$). Furthermore, DANN reduces reliance on both the spurious shortcut ($\Delta f = 0.155 \pm 0.267$) and the true causal feature ($\Delta f = 0.012 \pm 0.021$). The DANN model was trained using these examples only and tested using the entire set of drug-target pairs (Appendix \ref{sec:simulator}). CORAL preserves both signals nearly intact ($x_0 = 0.888 \pm 0.040$, $x_1 = 1.000 \pm 0.000$) and maintains the strong functional reliance on the spurious shortcut ($\Delta f = 0.988 \pm 0.009$ for $x_1$) while effectively reducing covariance distance between domains ($>$99.6\% reduction vs.\ Standard NN; Appendix \ref{sec:alignment}).

For each model seed we compute a seed-level mean efficacy $\bar{E}_s$ (Table \ref{tab:design}).

Under covariate shift, where the causal mechanism is shared across domains, CORAL and the Standard NN achieve near-ceiling efficacy ($1.000 \pm 0.000$ and $0.989 \pm 0.031$, respectively), while DANN remains high on average but more variable ($0.900 \pm 0.300$). Under mechanism shift, all source-trained oracles achieve low true efficacy. The aligned methods have lower mean efficacy than the unaligned oracle (DANN: $0.189 \pm 0.333$; CORAL: $0.213 \pm 0.290$; Standard NN: $0.264 \pm 0.296$), but the seed-level effects are variable and the paired differences are not statistically significant ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.770$ and $0.465$, respectively; two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, $n{=}10$ model seeds). This clean interaction was isolated by a difference-of-differences permutation test across model seeds ($p = 0.0039$).

Under constrained optimization in the covariate-shift regime, prior-penalized CORAL achieves significantly higher efficacy than the Standard NN ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.023$, mean $\Delta E = +0.116$, 95\% CI $[-0.029, 0.261]$). Under mechanism shift with the exact same optimizer, CORAL achieves significantly lower true efficacy than the Standard NN (9/10 seeds negative, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.008$, mean $\Delta E = -0.094$, 95\% CI $[-0.230, 0.043]$). The DANN result remains non-significant due to high variance from outlier seeds. A projection-based constraint ($\|Z\| \leq 4.0$) produces a similar pattern but without significance (Appendix details available in supplementary code). All constrained candidates remain R/K-enriched regardless of oracle, confirming that decoded peptide diversity is limited by the synthetic VAE training data. This clean interaction (difference-of-differences permutation test across model seeds, $p = 0.0039$) isolates mechanism shift as the cause of the CORAL degradation.


\label{sec:discussion}
\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The positive control reveals that with the same mechanism for source and target, CORAL and the Standard NN attain close to ceiling efficacy, while all source-trained methods suffer degradation upon mechanism shift. The most significant result comes from the representation probes. While CORAL nearly completely removes the covariance discrepancy ($>$99.6\% reduction; Appendix \ref{sec:alignment}), the shortcut remains fully decodable ($x_1$ AUC = 1.000). When using an independent domain probe, DANN's representations are still perfectly domain separable (probe AUC = 1.000; Appendix \ref{sec:alignment}). We also observe DANN diminishing the causal signal ($x_0$ AUC: $0.829 \to 0.651$) without erasing the shortcut information ($x_1$ AUC = 0.993). With prior penalization in optimization restricting the extent of radial latent extrapolation, the CORAL oracle consistently suffers in design ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.008$, 9/10 seeds negative), showing that the CORAL versus Standard difference persists in the prior regularized latent region. The fundamental problem here is not just bad domain adaptation; it is the known issue of conditional shift breaking invariant UDA. Invariant UDA attempts to enforce invariance between domains whose underlying causal mechanism has shifted \cite{zhao2019learning}.

\textbf{Limitations.} First, this is a controlled semi-synthetic stress test, not peptide-engineering validation. While we simulate pathogen features, domains, and drug-target efficacy, BV-BRC data only provides resistance prevalence. We deliberately violate assumptions of shared labels \cite{zhao2019learning} by intentionally introducing mechanism shift. Decoded candidates still converge to R/K-rich sequences even with prior-penalized optimization ($\|Z\| \approx 2$) (Appendix \ref{sec:vae_validity}). Finally, the VAE does not have the capacity to generate diverse physicochemical outputs. Thus, we take this design experiment as an oracle-reliability benchmark indicating a particular risk pathway when biological oracles are optimized under mechanism shift, rather than evidence of real AMR pipelines being unsafe for clinical practice. In addition to biological oracles, real genomic features, realistic AMP data, and experimental validation remain necessary. Code and simulator specification are provided at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/AMR-519B}; training details are in Appendix \ref{sec:appendix}.

\section{Architecture and Hyperparameters} \label{sec:appendix}
Feature extractor architecture is the same across all deep learning models (Standard NN, DANN, CORAL): 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with 32 and 16 hidden units (ReLU activation); classification head is a single linear layer projecting to 1 dimension; domain discriminator for the DANN corresponds to classification head (i.e., a single linear layer), preceded by a Gradient Reversal Layer. The original implementation of Ganin et al. (2016) is followed to anneal the adversarial penalty weight from 0 to $\lambda$ during training as follows: $\lambda_p = \lambda \left(\frac{2}{1+\exp(-10\cdot p)} - 1\right)$ where $p$ is increased linearly from 0 to 1. The CORAL penalty calculates the squared Frobenius norm between the covariance matrices of the source and target embeddings, weighted by a fixed value of 1.0. The models are trained for 250 epochs using Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.005, weight decay of $1 \times 10^{-4}$, and BCE loss.

The Character-Level VAE consists of an embedding layer (21 tokens $\to$ 32-dim) followed by two 1D convolutions (64, 128 channels) and projects to a 16-dimensional latent space ($\mu$, $\log \sigma^2$). The decoder matches it with a linear layer, two transposed convolutions, and softmax output. The models are trained for 10 epochs (Adam, lr$= 10^{-3}$, $\beta_{\text{KL}} = 0.1$) on 5,000 synthetic AMP sequences. Length is drawn uniformly from $[15, 30]$ to generate the sequence. Residues at each position are selected with probability 30\% that they will be a cationic residue (R, K), 40\% chance that they will be a hydrophobic residue (L, I, V, W, F, A), otherwise they are drawn uniformly randomly from all 20 standard amino acids. The Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity scale is used to calculate the properties listed in Appendix \ref{sec:vae_validity}.

Design oracles are separately trained on each of 10 model seeds. Each model seed has 10 matched latent initializations. Each latent initialization is shared by all four oracle types. Hyperparameters ($\lambda = 1.0$, CORAL weight$= 1.0$) are held constant a priori. Target labels are used only for final evaluation.

\section{Complete Simulator Specification} \label{sec:simulator}
For each sample $i$, with assigned domain $c_i \in \{0,1\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $x_0 \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.402)$.
    \item $y \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.761)$ if $x_0{=}1$, else $y \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.090)$.
    \item If $c{=}0$: $x_1 \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.99)$ if $y{=}1$, else $\text{Bernoulli}(0.01)$; $x_{2..9} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.9)$; $P(x_{10..17}{=}1) = 0$.
    \item If $c{=}1$: $x_1 \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.1)$ (independent of $y$); $x_{10..17} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.9)$; $P(x_{2..9}{=}1) = 0$.
    \item $x_{18..49} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(0.1)$ (global noise).
\end{enumerate}
Peptide latent vectors $Z_{\text{amp}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ are sampled i.i.d.\ from the standard normal prior; one vector is assigned per bacterial sample (3,975 pairs total).

\section{Alignment Metrics} \label{sec:alignment}
To ensure that our alignment goals are being achieved, we provide:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DANN domain-classifier accuracy} (final epoch): $0.798 \pm 0.240$ across 10 model seeds.
    \item \textbf{Independent DANN domain probe}: A logistic regression classifier trained post-hoc on frozen DANN encoder embeddings (evaluated on a held-out 30\% test split of embeddings, stratified by domain) to predict source vs.\ target domain achieves AUC $= 1.000 \pm 0.000$ across all 10 seeds. The DANN representation remains perfectly domain-separable despite adversarial training.
    \item \textbf{CORAL covariance distance}: Standard NN $0.260 \pm 0.070$ vs.\ CORAL $0.0008 \pm 0.0004$ ($>$99.6\% reduction).
\end{itemize}
CORAL successfully reduces second-order covariance discrepancy without removing the shortcut. The clean interpretation is that DANN's adversarial training does not reliably remove domain information, leaving causal-feature decodability while also rendering the domain and shortcut features fully recoverable.
